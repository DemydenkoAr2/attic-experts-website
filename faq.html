Insulation

It depends on your attic structure, climate zone, moisture exposure, and goals. We inspect first and recommend after – blown-in cellulose, fiberglass, spray foam, or batts. We'll never push a material that doesn't make sense for your situation.

In most cases yes – if existing insulation is dry and in good condition. Removal is needed when there's contamination, moisture damage, or rodent activity. We'll tell you honestly which applies after the inspection.

California code for most Bay Area attics is R-38. Many energy programs and BayREN rebates require R-44 or higher. We measure your current depth during inspection and recommend the right target for your home and goals.

Spray foam costs more upfront but also air-seals – something blown-in doesn't do. For attics with irregular framing, lots of penetrations, or persistent drafts, spray foam often makes more sense long-term. We'll walk through the trade-offs for your specific attic.

Rodent Proofing

Listen for scratching or scurrying sounds – especially at night. Check for droppings, gnaw marks on wiring or wood, nesting material, or a persistent musty smell. If you notice any of these, schedule a free inspection.

Our focus is sealing every entry point with chew-resistant materials and removing contaminated insulation. For active rodent removal, we can advise on or coordinate with a pest control partner.

Yes – we offer a workmanship guarantee on all sealed entry points. If rodents get back in through a point we sealed, we come back. Ask us about the specific terms during your estimate.

After the Job

Most customers notice a meaningful difference – rooms that were always too hot or cold even out, and heating/cooling runs less frequently. The impact depends on how under-insulated you were before, your home's size, and window quality.

Blown-in cellulose and fiberglass typically last 20–30 years. Spray foam can last the lifetime of the building. The main reason insulation needs early replacement is contamination – rodents, moisture, or mold – not material failure.
