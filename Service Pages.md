## Spray Foam Insulation

### H1
Spray Foam Insulation in Bay Area

### Hero Subtitle
Spray foam insulates and air-seals in a single application — one of the most effective options for attics with tight corners, irregular framing, or persistent drafts.

### What Is Spray Foam Insulation
Spray foam is a liquid insulation that expands on contact and hardens into a solid layer, filling gaps and cavities that rigid materials can't reach. Unlike batt or blown-in insulation, it also acts as an air barrier — so it insulates and seals leaks at the same time.

### When You Need It
- Noticeable drafts or uneven temperatures between rooms
- High heating/cooling bills despite existing insulation
- An attic with irregular framing, tight spaces, or lots of penetrations (vents, wiring, plumbing)
- You want a long-term solution and don't plan to touch the attic again for decades

### How We Do It
1. Free on-site inspection — we assess your attic's structure, current insulation, and air leaks
2. Prep — living space is sealed off and protected, safety gear and ventilation set up
3. Application — certified techs apply open-cell or closed-cell foam based on your attic's needs
4. Cure & walkthrough — foam cures, we ventilate, then walk the space with you and clean up

### FAQ

**Is spray foam worth it compared to blown-in?**
Spray foam costs more upfront than blown-in, but it lasts significantly longer and stops air leaks that blown-in doesn't — so the gap often closes over time through lower energy bills. We'll walk through the trade-offs for your specific attic during the free estimate.

**Can I stay home during installation?**
Yes, but the treated area needs to ventilate and cure before use — we'll walk you through exact timing and any off-gassing precautions for your job.

**Open-cell or closed-cell — which do I need?**
Depends on your climate goals, moisture exposure, and budget priorities. Closed-cell is denser and adds structural rigidity; open-cell is lighter and better for sound dampening. We'll recommend one during inspection, not sell you the pricier option by default.

## Blown-In Insulation

### H1
Blown-In Insulation in Bay Area

### Hero Subtitle
Fast, thorough coverage for Bay Area attics — blown-in insulation reaches corners and gaps that are hard to insulate by hand.

### What Is Blown-In Insulation
Blown-in insulation is loose material — cellulose or fiberglass — distributed into your attic using specialized equipment. It settles evenly into corners, around obstructions, and between joists, giving consistent coverage across the whole space.

### When You Need It
- Existing insulation has settled, thinned, or left gaps
- You want fast, whole-attic coverage without extensive prep
- Your attic has irregular framing or obstructions that are hard to insulate manually
- You're topping up existing insulation rather than starting from scratch

### How We Do It
1. Free inspection — assess current insulation depth, material, and coverage gaps
2. Prep — work area protected, equipment staged for access through the attic hatch
3. Blow-in — material distributed evenly using calibrated equipment to reach target depth
4. Depth check & walkthrough — coverage verified against target R-value, area cleaned before we leave

### FAQ

**Can blown-in go over my old insulation?**
In most cases, yes — if your existing insulation is dry and in reasonable condition, we can blow in on top of it. We'll flag it during inspection if removal is needed first.

**How do I know my current R-value is too low?**
Common signs: visible gaps between insulation and joists, insulation that looks compressed or discolored, or rooms that never feel consistently comfortable. We measure exact depth during the free inspection.

**What's the difference between cellulose and fiberglass blown-in?**
Cellulose is treated recycled paper fiber — denser, better at filling small gaps. Fiberglass is spun glass fiber that holds its loft longer over time. We'll recommend the better fit for your attic.

### What Is Insulation Removal
The process of extracting old, damaged, or contaminated insulation from an attic or crawl space using vacuum equipment, leaving a clean, safe surface behind — whether you're reinsulating right away or not.

### When You Need It
- Insulation shows signs of rodent droppings, nesting, or odor

4. Disposal & walkthrough — material disposed of properly, attic inspected and left ready for next steps

### FAQ

**Do I have to remove old insulation before adding new?**
Not always — if your existing insulation is clean and in good condition, we can often add to it. Removal is needed when there's contamination, damage, or material has lost its effectiveness. We'll tell you honestly which applies to your attic.

**How do you dispose of old insulation?**
It's bagged and contained during removal, then hauled off and disposed of according to local waste regulations — nothing is left behind in your attic or yard.

**Is old insulation dangerous?**
It can be, depending on age and condition — older material may contain asbestos, and contaminated insulation can carry mold spores or rodent waste. We handle accordingly and flag any health-related concerns before starting work.

## Rodent Proofing

### H1
Rodent Proofing in Bay Area

### Hero Subtitle
Rodents don't just damage your insulation — they contaminate it. We seal every entry point with materials they can't chew through.

### What Is Rodent Proofing
A systematic inspection and sealing process that closes off every way rodents can get into your attic or crawl space — using steel mesh, metal flashing, and other chew-resistant materials at vents, pipe penetrations, eaves, and foundation gaps.

### When You Need It
- Scratching or scurrying sounds in the attic or walls
- Droppings, nesting material, or a persistent odor in the attic
- Visible chew marks on wiring, ductwork, or wood framing
- You've had rodent problems before and want to prevent them from returning

### How We Do It
1. Free inspection — walk the exterior and attic to identify every current and potential entry point
2. Sealing — steel mesh and metal flashing installed at vents, gaps, pipes, and foundation openings
3. Verification — each sealed point checked to confirm it's fully secured
4. Walkthrough — we show you exactly what was sealed and where

### FAQ

**How do I know if I have rodents in my attic?**
Listen for movement sounds (especially at night), check for droppings or gnaw marks, and watch for a persistent musty odor. If you notice any of these, it's worth scheduling an inspection.

**Does rodent proofing come with a guarantee?**
Yes — ask us about our workmanship guarantee on sealed entry points during your estimate.

**Do you remove the rodents or just seal entry points?**
Our focus is sealing entry points and, where needed, removing contaminated insulation. For active rodent removal, we can advise or coordinate with a pest control partner.

## Crawl Space Insulation

### H1
Crawl Space Insulation in Bay Area

### Hero Subtitle
An uninsulated crawl space can account for a significant share of your home's heat loss. We insulate and seal crawl spaces to improve comfort and prevent moisture problems.

### What Is Crawl Space Insulation
Installing insulation — and, where needed, a vapor barrier — along the walls or floor of your crawl space to reduce heat loss and control moisture, improving comfort in the rooms above.

### When You Need It
- Cold floors in rooms above the crawl space
- Musty odor or visible moisture/condensation in the crawl space
- No or damaged existing insulation down there
- Signs of mold or mildew on framing or subfloor

### How We Do It
1. Free inspection — assess moisture levels, existing insulation, and ventilation
2. Prep — clear debris and address any moisture issues before insulating
3. Installation — insulation and vapor barrier installed per your crawl space's needs
4. Final walkthrough — coverage confirmed and any ongoing moisture-control steps explained

### FAQ

**Will this fix cold floors in my house?**
In most cases, yes — crawl space insulation is one of the most effective ways to address cold floors caused by heat loss from below.

**Do I need a vapor barrier too?**
Often, yes — especially in crawl spaces with any visible moisture. We'll assess this during inspection and recommend accordingly.

**How do I know if I have moisture problems down there?**
Signs include a musty smell, condensation on pipes or framing, or visible mold. We check this as part of the free inspection.
